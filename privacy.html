Long Bloody Road

Privacy

Last updated 24 November 2026

Long Bloody Road is a WW2 tactics game for iPhone and iPad. This page says what the game and this website do with your information. The short answer is that the game never learns who you are.

What the game collects

The game has no accounts and no sign-in. It shows no adverts. It never asks for your name, your email address or your location, and it does not track you across other apps or websites.

Anonymous usage data

The version of the game that adds Africa's Dust, and every version after it, sends us a small amount of anonymous information about how the game is played, so we can see which missions people finish and where they stop. Earlier versions send nothing.

It records that the game was opened, that a mission was started, finished or left, with its name, its difficulty and whether it was won, and whether a purchase or a restore went through. Each report carries a random number made up on your device, so that reports from the same copy of the game can be counted together. That number is not your name, your Apple Account or anything else that says who you are.

The reports go to PostHog, an analytics service, and are kept on its servers in the European Union. PostHog is set not to keep the internet address a report came from. We do not sell this information or share it with anyone else.

You can turn this off at any time: open Settings in the game and switch off Share usage data. Nothing more is sent after that.

Your saved games

Your campaign, your men and your settings are saved on your own device, in the game's own storage. They are never sent to us. Deleting the game deletes them. They may be included in your own device backups, which are run by you and Apple.

Purchases

Hill 192 and Operation Cobra are bought through Apple's App Store. Apple handles the payment. We never see your name, your card or your Apple Account. The game is told only whether the two missions are unlocked. Apple's own privacy policy covers the purchase: apple.com/legal/privacy.

Email news from this website

If you give your email address on this website to hear about new campaigns, it is kept by Buttondown, the service that sends the emails, and used only for that. We send one email for each new campaign. Every email has an unsubscribe link, and unsubscribing removes you from the list. The game itself never asks for an email address.

Children

The game treats everyone the same, whatever their age: it collects nothing that says who a player is.

Changes to this page

If the game ever changes in a way that affects your privacy, this page will be updated before the change is released, with a new date at the top.

Questions about privacy: support@longbloodyroad.com.
